Verification mark

The verification mark is syoksheet's most important signal — it means a real person or a DNS-verified organisation stood behind a brag. It is flat, quiet, and reserved exclusively for proven things.

Beta forest green · semantic

Principle

Trust is the product, so the mark earns it by being precise and consistent — not loud. It is a plain forest-green check, the way a tick on a legal document works. No stamp, no wax, no ring text, no gradient.

Forest green (--verify-600) is reserved for verification only. It is never used for brand, action, or decoration. The brand colour is teal; these two greens never mix roles.

Variants

One shape, three weights. Solid leads, soft and outline support in dense or low-emphasis contexts.

In context

The mark almost always travels with a label. Colour alone never carries the meaning.

Verified by Acme Corp 3 peers Pending Unverified
Verified = mark + label. Pending = hollow amber clock. Unverified = neutral grey, no mark.

The status system

Verification is one of three mutually exclusive states. Each has its own colour and glyph so they're never confused.

StateColourGlyphMeaning
VerifiedForest --verify-600Solid checkA person or DNS-verified org confirmed this.
PendingAmber --amber-600Hollow clockAwaiting a verifier's response.
UnverifiedNeutral --n-600NoneDocumented but not vouched for.

Do & don't

Do
Verified by Acme Corp

Mark beside a label that names who verified.

Don't

Forest green on a non-verification action — it steals the trust signal.

Accessibility

  • The mark is decorative when a text label is present (aria-hidden); the label carries meaning.
  • When the mark stands alone (e.g. a card-corner check), give it an aria-label="Verified".
  • Never communicate verified / pending / unverified by colour alone — glyph + label always accompany.
  • Forest-on-white and white-on-forest both meet WCAG AA.
