Toast

A toast is a brief, self-dismissing confirmation that an action succeeded (or failed). It floats above the page, never blocks it, and disappears on its own. For a message that must persist in the layout, use an Alert.

Beta bottom-right

Anatomy

A compact dark pill: a small status mark, a one-line message, and an optional single action (usually Undo) or dismiss. Dark surface keeps it distinct from page content and readable over anything.

Tones & action

Always dark; the small status mark carries the tone. A toast may offer exactly one action — most often Undo for a reversible change.

Do & don't

Do

One line, one optional action. Confirm what happened ("Brag verified") and let it fade.

Don't

Don't put critical info, multiple actions, or anything the user must read in a toast — it vanishes. Use an alert or modal.

Content

  • Past tense, one clause — "Draft saved", "Invite sent", "Brag hidden".
  • No title/body split — a toast is a single line. If it needs two, it's an alert.
  • Pair reversible actions with Undo, irreversible failures with Retry.

Accessibility

  • Live region: success/info use role="status" (polite); errors use role="alert" (assertive).
  • Auto-dismiss pauses on hover and on keyboard focus, so it's never missed.
  • Any action (Undo/Retry) is a real focusable <button>; the toast is reachable before it dismisses.
  • Dark surface meets AA contrast for white text and all three status marks.
